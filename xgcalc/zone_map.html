What the comparison says

  • It ranks very slightly worse, and calibrates clearly better. AUC 0.7106 vs 0.7139 for the hand-cut 10 — a rounding error — but total xG lands within 1.2% of actual goals instead of 4.6%. If you are summing xG over a game or a player, the EDGE map is the better tool; if you are ranking individual chances, they are the same tool.
  • It trades distance resolution for angle resolution. Three rings instead of four leaves shots 4.7 ft from their zone's median distance on average (vs 4.1 ft), but the radial cuts leave them only 9.3° off in angle (vs 13.0°). Distance is the stronger term in the model, which is exactly why the AUC slips a hair.
  • The left/right split is free, and buys nothing. Collapsing all six L/R pairs into 11 distinct shapes costs 0.0003 AUC. Keep the split for readability and for handedness work later, but it is not carrying predictive weight.
  • No dead branches, and the tails are covered. Behind the goal line (1,777 shots) and the neutral zone (2,859 shots, 4.8% goal rate — empty nets and long shots) both get their own zones instead of being swept into a 33k-shot perimeter bucket.
  • Boundaries are digitised from your image, not from a spec. Rings, rays and the |y| = 17 split were measured off the picture against rink landmarks. Across 108 plausible readings (rings 20–26 / 40–50 ft, rays 15–25° / 40–50°) AUC spans 0.7021–0.7157, so the verdict above holds for any of them. The one knob that matters is the outer ring: pulling it from 45 to 40 ft is worth about +0.003 AUC. If you have the real EDGE numbers, they go in the constants at the top of zone_tagging.py and everything here regenerates.
